# Incident Reports

This folder contains incident reports developed as part of my cybersecurity learning journey, covering both Blue Team and Red Team activities, including SOC analysis, ethical hacking, and incident response home-lab projects.
The purpose of these reports is to practice documenting security incidents in a structured and professional format, based on evidence collected during investigations.

## 🎯 Purpose

Creating incident reports helps me develop the ability to:

- Document security incidents clearly and professionally
- Summarize technical findings for different audiences
- Identify and document Indicators of Compromise (IOCs)
- Build incident timelines
- Analyze the root cause of an incident
- Assess potential impact
- Document containment, eradication, and recovery procedures
- Provide security recommendations based on investigation findings
- Communicate technical investigation results in a structured report

## 📝 Report Structure

My incident reports generally follow a structure similar to a real-world security incident report:

1. **Executive Summary**
   - Incident overview
   - Severity
   - Status
   - Key findings
   - Immediate actions
   - Stakeholder impact

2. **Technical Analysis**
   - Affected systems
   - Evidence sources
   - Investigation findings
   - Indicators of Compromise (IOCs)
   - Root cause analysis
   - Technical timeline
   - Attack techniques and procedures

3. **Impact Analysis**
   - Business impact
   - System impact
   - Potential data exposure
   - Operational and reputational impact

4. **Response and Recovery**
   - Containment
   - Access revocation
   - Eradication
   - Vulnerability remediation
   - System recovery
   - Post-incident monitoring

5. **Lessons Learned**
   - Security gaps
   - Recommended improvements
   - Future security strategy

## 🔎 Evidence-Based Reporting

I aim to distinguish between information directly observed in logs and conclusions derived from the investigation.

When documenting an incident, I try to separate:

- **Observed evidence** — What the logs or other evidence directly show
- **Analysis** — What the evidence indicates
- **Assessment** — Conclusions based on multiple pieces of evidence
- **Threat intelligence** — Additional information used to enrich the investigation
- **Recommended response** — Actions that could be taken to contain and recover from the incident

This helps prevent assumptions from being presented as confirmed facts.

## 🧪 Home Lab Disclaimer

Some reports in this folder are based on cybersecurity training datasets and home-lab exercises rather than real-world incidents.

When response, containment, eradication, or recovery actions are hypothetical, they are clearly identified as simulated recommendations rather than actions actually performed on a production system.
